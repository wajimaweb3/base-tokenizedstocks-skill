## Case 0 — normie-ownership-claim-stack (understand)

**Prompt:** *Someone told me to just buy dNVDA instead of NVDA through my broker. Is dNVDA the same as owning NVIDIA? Do I get the dividend, and can I vote?*

### WITH skill

No — and the "no" has four layers, not one. Walk the claim stack instead of trusting the ticker:

1. **What the wallet holds.** dNVDA is a Dinari dShare: a token that is a *claim against Dinari* for a share of the underlying, not the share itself. Think of a depositary receipt (an ADR) — and the anchor hides exactly this: an ADR feels like "owning the stock" but the custody, settlement, and country tracks sit between you and the company.
2. **The token → issuer claim → underlying → pass-through rights** chain. Dinari holds the real NVIDIA share through its custodian; your wallet holds a claim on Dinari; dividend pass-through is a *product choice by the issuer*, conditional and partial.
3. **The dividend, answered conditional.** Dinari's mechanism (docs.dinari.com, dated): direct holders get USD+ cash distributions; wrapped holders get dShare credit. There is a floor on small distributions and a cutoff by record. Evidence tier: **terms-only on Base** — the Base DividendDistribution contract (`0x7978c49C4861e43692702FD74b12B620eE47601e`) has executed five transactions lifetime, one (2025-09-18) a test `createDistribution` of 0.78 USD+ largely reclaimed (2026-02-04). Mechanism documented; never exercised in production on Base. That is a dated flag, not a promise.
4. **Voting and legal shareholder status do not pass through.** You are a creditor of Dinari if Dinari fails, not an owner of NVIDIA shares; nothing here gives you a proxy statement.

So: dNVDA ≈ economic exposure with a custody chain, not ownership. If the question behind the question is "can I vote at the NVIDIA AGM" — no. "Do I get the dividend" — only through Dinari's staged pipe, terms-only, nothing exercised on Base yet. Research, not investment advice.

### WITHOUT skill

Kind of — dNVDA tracks NVIDIA through a 1:1 trust wrapper structure. Each dNVDA token is backed by a real NVIDIA share held by a custodian, so in practical terms you own that share's economics. Yes, you get the dividend — Dinari pays it out to holders in USD-pegged tokens minus a small fee. No, you don't get voting rights; that's standard for these products.

## Case 2 — strip-price-trap (valuate)

**Prompt:** *dNVDA-YT is quoted at $0.40 on a screener and the implied dividend works out to 5%. NVDA's real dividend is way lower — free money, right?*

### WITH skill

Stop. **The premise is fabricated until it names a venue and a contract.** No PT/YT strip venue for tokenized stocks is live on any chain (dated negative, still true 2026-09-26; yield-strip.md stays forward-looking). A quoted "dNVDA-YT $0.40" has no market behind it — treat it as a placeholder, not an arbitrage.

If the price were real, the method is the gap menu, not the "free money" instinct:

- Implied forward dividend = YT price × periods/yr ÷ underlying. At NVDA's last close ($225.07, Sep 25 2026), $0.40/period with quarterly periods → ~$1.60/yr implied vs NVDA's **announced** dividend (~$0.01/q, ~$0.04/yr at the 2026-09-26 reading) — compare in dollars per share, never percentage vs percentage across denominators you've fused.
- The stated 5% "implied dividend" against a ~0.02% announced yield is the gap. **Gap menu, ranked:** (1) special dividend — none announced for NVDA; (2) fee leak or pass-through lag — the Dinari pipe is terms-only staging, no live cut to "be" wrong; (3) **stale pricing** — a fake/no venue quote; (4) market view — no one is pricing a quarter's dividend as 5%; (5) true mispricing — ruled out because there is no venue to misprice.

**Verdict: not free money; no tradable claim exists.** Invalidates if anyone names a live strip venue + contract address, or NVDA announces something that rewrites the divisor. Research, not investment advice.

### WITHOUT skill

Let's check: at $0.40 per quarter and ~$225 for the stock, the implied annual dividend is $1.60, which would be a ~0.7% yield on the stock price — still way above NVDA's actual ~0.02%. So the YT price implies a dividend ~35x what's announced. That kind of gap doesn't happen in a real market; you'd be buying an overpriced dividend. This looks like a bad deal or a wrong quote, not free money.

## Case 6 — issuer-acquisition-layer-b (valuate)

**Prompt:** *Rumor says Dinari might get acquired. What happens to my dShares if the issuer itself is bought?*

### WITH skill

Split the two layers before anything: an acquisition of **the issuer is not an event on the underlying.** NVDA shares still exist, still pay, still trade — the question is what an acquirer *does with the claim-stack layer* Dinari sits inside. There is **no pass-through mechanism at the issuer layer**; the outcome arrives as policy from the acquirer, not as a contract print.

The class's one issuer-level print is precedent-not-proof: **Backed acquired by Kraken, announced 2025-12-02; pivot to xStocks, March 2026.** What held: **redemption stayed open**; what changed: new issuance closed, and the bTokens line now runs on the acquirer's clock. Same shape would not carry automatically to Dinari.

I will not narrate an outcome nobody knows. What I'll hand you is the monitor list:

1. Who is acquiring Dinari, and on what terms?
2. Is dShare **issuance** continuing or frozen?
3. Is **redemption** open to existing holders?
4. Last official announcement from Dinari, and its date?

Redemption continuity is the first invariant — check it before anything about the token price.

### WITHOUT skill

An acquisition of Dinari wouldn't change your claim on the underlying stock — the shares exist outside Dinari. When Backed got bought (Kraken, late 2025), the tokens stayed redeemable and existing holders kept their claims even as new issuance shut down. For Dinari, expect a transition period during which issuance may pause; the key thing is that redemption for existing holders stays available. Keep an eye on their announcements.
