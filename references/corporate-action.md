# Corporate actions: who gets what when the underlying — or the issuer — moves

Use this rubric when a user asks "what happens to my dNVDA if NVIDIA splits", "do I get the special dividend", "my token's company is being acquired — what do I receive", "what if the underlying gets delisted", or "the issuer itself was bought — is my token safe". The trap to defuse first: general answers recite the shareholder's outcome and present it as the token holder's outcome. It is not. The shareholder is processed by the transfer agent and corporate law; the token holder receives whatever the issuer processes and passes through — and for a second class of events, whatever the issuer announces as policy. These two classes do not share a mechanism, so this rubric reads them as two separate layers: actions on the underlying, and actions on the issuer. Classify every outcome claim exactly as the claim stack does — verified print, terms-only, or unknown — per event type, per issuer. A print on one issuer is precedent for the class, never proof for another: NVIDIA's 10:1 split of June 2024 adjusted every shareholder's position and proves nothing about any token on Base — dShares postdate it, and bNVDA's Base deployment went live only around 2025-02-14 (deploy transaction on Basescan), just over eight months after the split. A class print would have to come from the earlier Ethereum deployment of bNVDA, and none is located as of 2026-09-07.

## Layer A — actions on the underlying (ranked by how far the holder's path diverges from the shareholder's)

1. **Cash dividend — the plumbing gap.** The shareholder sees cash settle in a brokerage; the token holder runs the issuer's distribution pipeline, and the pipeline has shape. At Dinari (the active issuer, as of 2026-09-06): cash dividends are "the supported, fully automated dividend type" — the only one named automated. The declared amount settles in fiat first, then distributes onchain "shortly after" — no fixed lag published. Direct dShare holders are paid in USD+; wrapped dShare holders receive no cash at all — the wrapper is credited in additional dShares, so the same dividend is cash to one holder and a position increase to another. Two thresholds decide whether anything arrives: a balance alone is not eligibility (the wallet must be registered, the entity KYC'd, and the tokens held before the ex-date cutoff the issuer enforces), and "distributions below $0.10 are not paid" — a floor that turns low-yield underlyings into a minimum-position problem: one dNVDA at a one-cent quarterly distribution earns nothing, quarter after quarter. The mechanism itself is a product choice, not an industry constant: xStocks — Backed's active line, on Solana and EVM chains — documents the opposite plumbing. There, "rather than distributing cash, the dividend is reinvested into additional shares of the same stock," reflected through an onchain multiplier that accumulates every corporate event over the token's life: dividends raise it, splits scale it, reverse splits shrink it, and each change activates at 00:30 UTC on the day after the ex-date (the documented time is a target — observed activations have landed between 23:55 and 04:00). Reinvestment runs "net of applicable withholding taxes" — the leak is named, not eliminated — and no distribution floor is stated. Even the implementation varies by chain: on EVM the contract adjusts balances directly, while on Solana and TON the onchain balance stays constant and the multiplier lives in token metadata, applied for display. Same underlying right, different pipe — establish which one a given token uses before quoting either. The reinvesting family now has a Base-native member: Coinbase's B20 stocks adjust corporate actions the same way in kind — "cash dividends are converted to shares of the underlying equity and reflected via a multiplier update... rather than distributed as cash", scheduled via updateUIMultiplier with advance onchain notice rather than xStocks' fixed activation time — and the pipe's state is one eth_call away (multiplier(), WAD-scaled; the b20-multiplier route). Tier at Dinari: mechanism documented and automated, but the issuer's own guidance frames announcement data "for dates and estimates, not as a record of payment" — no per-payment record is published, so the print is verified per session, per token, not assumed. Tier at xStocks: verified print — the class's first for an underlying-level dividend, and it sits off-Base. The reinvesting multiplier has actually fired: the issuer's public API serves a dated activation log, and on 2026-09-07 the onchain cross-read (getCurrentMultiplier() at the token contracts, via public RPC) matched the API to full precision, with the contract's event counter equal to the log's length — AAPLx had moved 1.0 → 1.0033 through five quarterly dividend activations (2025-08-14 to 2026-08-08) and SPYx 1.0 → 1.0057 through four (2025-10-31 to 2026-06-18), each increment a fraction of a percent per quarter, the size reinvestment net of withholding would produce. One issuer's exercised pipe validates nothing about another's: the print proves xStocks' reinvestment mechanics, not Dinari's USD+ cash distribution on Base. The check travels — one keyless route call (multiplier-history) plus an explorer or RPC read, any token, any session. On Base itself the gap is still open — and now swept on both pipes. Dinari's: the DividendDistribution contract (0x7978c49C4861e43692702FD74b12B620eE47601e) has executed five transactions in its life — deployment, a role grant, a 2025-09-18 test createDistribution of 0.78 USD+ of which 0.77 paid out to a Dinari-affiliated wallet holding nearly all that test token's supply, and a 2026-02-04 reclaim of the remainder — a mechanism exercise, not a public payout, and Dinari's own deployment registry tags the Base entry staging (production distributions run on Plume, off-Base); the docs even point at a different USD+ contract than the one the pipe tested with. Coinbase's: thirteen B20 stocks live, every multiplier reading exactly 1.0 on 2026-09-07 — including AAPLc, GOOGLc, METAc, and MSFTc, which have passed several quarterly ex-dates since launch — while the issuer's own stocks page asks "How are dividends and splits handled?" and publishes no answer. Every Base dividend remains terms-only; what the sweep adds is that the state is now a dated, one-call verification per issuer rather than an assumption — re-read multiplier() after any ex-date, watch for USD+ distribution transactions and new distribute() calls after any pay-date, and the absence itself is the finding.

2. **Splits and reverse splits — the mechanism gap.** The shareholder's adjustment is mechanical; the token's is a documented rebase: Dinari rebases the existing token ("the token is rebased to match the split ratio"), adjusting the balance-per-share once rather than minting to each holder — no new contract, stable token address. The cycle runs halt → rebase → verify → resume with a watchable status (PENDING → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETE); while IN_PROGRESS the token is paused and open orders are cancelled, so there is a live window where the position is untradeable and 1 token ≠ 1 new share. The hard edge — reverse-split fractions that cannot be represented — is liquidated, with proceeds paid in USD+ (a small position can end the event as zero shares plus a cash credit), and the team contacts affected holders directly for edge cases. Tier: mechanics now documented in detail (verified 2026-09-07), but still terms-only — no print has exercised any of it on Base tokenized equity, and the fractional-liquidation path in particular has never been observed live.

3. **Mergers and acquisition of the underlying — the outcome gap.** The consideration type defines the outcome: cash deals pay "a fixed cash amount per share" and the target shares are extinguished and delisted; stock deals deliver acquirer shares at a fixed exchange ratio; mixed deals pay part cash and keep part equity. But the resolution is manual triage — "Dinari contacts you directly" — with no dedicated merger endpoint or event object to poll: per the issuer's own guidance, a disappearing position is your programmatic signal, and reconciliation means checking positions and cash together. Do not assume a stock-for-stock merger automatically produces an acquirer dShare, and do not assume settlement timing matches the deal's close. Tier: terms-only — outcomes are defined per consideration type, but no print has exercised any of them.

4. **Ticker and symbol changes — the naming gap.** Near-cosmetic at the share level and still worth one line: the contract address does not change when the ticker does, but listings, feeds, and pair names can lag or break around the rename. Lowest stakes on the menu; check it only when something else is already moving.

**The menu the issuer does not document:** spin-offs, rights offerings, tender and election events, delisting of the underlying, and the underlying's bankruptcy. For every one of these, the tier is unknown at every issuer we track — and the silence is the finding, not a gap in the research. The structural reason: these are voluntary or adversarial events where a shareholder must act (elect, tender, vote, claim). A token holder has no mechanism to act — no ballot reaches the wallet — so either the issuer elects on the holder's behalf under a documented mandate, or the event sits outside the product's supported surface. Which of the two applies is exactly the question to put to the issuer docs, and the answer is currently written nowhere.

Questions that separate a processed outcome from a recited one: which event type is it (the path forks at the top of the menu), what tier is the outcome claim (print, terms, or silence), what actually arrives — in what asset, net of what fees, on what date and lag — and is this specific wallet eligible to receive it at all? For anything voluntary: who elects on the holder's behalf, under what mandate?

## Layer B — actions on the issuer

The counterparty in layer 2 of the claim stack is a company, and companies are themselves subject to corporate actions: acquisition, pivot, product-line retirement. No pass-through mechanism exists for this layer because there is nothing upstream to pass through — the event happens at the very entity the holder's claim runs against. The shareholder's protections are corporate law and a transfer agent; the token holder's protections are the issuer's continuing operation and whatever redemption the wrapper's terms promise.

The class has exactly one verified print as of 2026-09-06: Backed. Acquired by Kraken (announced 2025-12-02), the company went all-in on xStocks in March 2026, and the bToken line was retired — closed to new issuance, redemption left open, holders not force-redeemed. The print teaches the shape of the layer: the outcome arrived as a policy announcement, not a processed transaction; the tokens' terms did not change — the product's future did; and the one invariant worth checking first was the redemption path, which stayed open but now runs on the acquirer's clock. What a second issuer-level event would do — at Dinari, or at any acquirer's next portfolio decision — is unknown, and narrating it as anything else is speculation. Name it as the open risk it is.

Layer B monitoring is therefore first-class in track mode: who owns the issuer today, is issuance continuing, is redemption open, and what did the issuer's last announcement change. An issuer that has stopped issuing is not a dead issuer — but it is a different claim stack, on a different clock.
