# Index composition: N claim stacks wearing one portfolio

Use this rubric when a user asks "build me a dividend basket of dShares", "what does my ten-token portfolio actually track", "is this AI-personalized index any good", "how do I rebalance without getting eaten by costs", "compare my basket against QQQ". The trap to defuse first: an onchain basket is not an index fund. It is N independent claim stacks plus one composition layer — and until a product says otherwise, the composition layer is a spreadsheet. The basket inherits the weakest link of every constituent: the thinnest exit, the strictest eligibility, the smallest dividend pass-through. A general answer treats the basket as one asset with a weighted price; the tokenized-stock answer reads N+1 things that can each fail separately.

The rubric runs in two parts. Part A applies live today to any wallet holding several dShares. Part B — automated vaults and AI-personalized index products — describes infrastructure no issuer or venue has shipped for tokenized stocks on Base as of 2026-09-07; treat any "index product" quoted today the way the strip rubric treats a quoted strip price: name the venue and the contract, or call it fabricated.

## Part A — the live case: a manual portfolio, analyzed as a composition

1. **Constituent screen — the basket is only as strong as its weakest claim stack.** Every constituent walks the four layers (claim-stack.md) before any weight is discussed: who issues it, is the line actively issued or retired, what is the peg quality, what dividend fraction survives the trip. One stale-peg or thin constituent contaminates the whole — the composition's tradability is gated by its least liquid member at the exact moment you rebalance.

2. **Weighting scheme and drift.** Equal-weight, market-cap, or theme weights — each carries a rebalancing implication. Prices move, weights drift, and a "dividend basket" silently becomes a momentum basket of whatever ran. State the weights dated, the drift since inception, and the band that would trigger a rebalance. A composition whose weights are only known at inception is not being managed; it is being observed.

3. **The dividend leg is a composition too.** Basket yield is the sum of (weight × net pass-through) across constituents — not the weighted headline yield, because each leg carries its own fraction, lag, and floor. The $0.10-per-distribution minimum (corporate-action.md) means small positions in low-yield names contribute exactly nothing: a basket of twenty half-positions can be structurally dividendless even while every constituent pays its shareholders. Do the dollar arithmetic per leg before quoting any basket-level yield.

4. **Rebalance cost and exit depth.** Rebalancing is N trades through the tokens' own liquidity, not one trade through an index. Size each adjustment against each constituent's pool depth (token-price route) — the rebalance is where backtests die. The sessions constrain execution too: order placement runs 24/5 with a weekend-eligible subset, so an adjustment planned for a Sunday only works for constituents in that subset.

5. **Eligibility intersection.** The composition is holdable only where every constituent is holdable: one restricted name redlines the whole basket for that holder. The binding constraint is the strictest member's KYC and regional rules, not the average.

6. **Tracking error against the stated theme.** Name the benchmark the basket claims to track — a sector, an index, a factor — and measure the divergence, dated. A composition that cannot name its benchmark is a vibe, not an index.

Questions that separate a composition from a pile of tickers: how many constituents are actively issued versus retired lines; what are the weights today versus inception; what fraction of the stated yield survives each leg's pass-through in dollars; which constituent has the thinnest exit at the holder's position size; whose eligibility is the binding constraint; and what benchmark does the composition claim, measured how, as of when?

## Part B — the forward case: automated composition (vaults, AI-personalized indexes)

No index, basket, or AI-personalized portfolio product for tokenized stocks is verified live on Base as of 2026-09-07 — but the layer beneath it has started arriving: Aerodrome stock/USDC pools carry active Beefy auto-compounding vaults (NVDAc, AAPLc, METAc, GOOGLc — verified via the Beefy API 2026-09-07), which are LP yield products, not compositions: they hold a trading pair, not a basket of claim stacks, so none of the composition questions below apply to them yet. The class's first live onchain stock index sits off-Base as evidence — Hood Index hMAG7 (Robinhood Chain, live 2026-07-17, seven MAG7 stocks, mint and redeem in-kind against an IndexVault, NAV via Chainlink, TVL ~$83 on 2026-09-07) — and the one announced Base-relevant candidate is not live: S&P Digital Markets 50 (Dinari × S&P DJI, announced 2025-10-08, "Dinari will create a token tracking the benchmark through dShares", no chain named, no contract as of 2026-09-07). The method, ready for the day one ships: the composition layer gains an operator, and the operator is the analysis. Who executes the rebalance, under what mandate, at what cadence? Can the operator change weights or fees unilaterally — and what did the terms say before entry? What does the wrapper charge on top of the N pass-throughs underneath (fee stacking: wrapper fee, plus each constituent's dividend leakage, plus rebalance costs paid at the thin-est exit)? Does the vault hold the tokens itself — one more claim layer, a claim on claims — or route through them at execution? Personalization adds the sharpest question: whose model picks the weights, on what data, retrained how often? An AI-personalized index is an actively managed fund with a non-human manager, and "personalized" is not a benchmark.

## Where the rest of the answer lives

Constituent-level depth: claim-stack.md per holding, corporate-action.md per event surface — and note that event exposure scales linearly in N, so a twenty-name basket is twenty dividend calendars and twenty issuer watch-lists in track mode; the track burden is part of the composition's cost whether or not anyone prices it. Strip legs, where they ever exist, re-enter through yield-strip.md. Data: the constituent-universe route for what exists, underlying-price per constituent, token-price for exit depth, dividend-calendar per yield leg. The skill composes analysis, never transactions — it names what a good composition would check, not what to click.
