# Lending against tokenized equity: what breaks first

Use this rubric when a user asks "can I borrow against my dNVDA", "should I take a loan against my dShares instead of selling", "what would my tokens collateralize", "can I be liquidated while the market is closed", "what is the collateral value of a YT". The trap to defuse first: borrowing against stock is a solved TradFi product — securities-based lending, where a custodian can deliver a share to anyone and the lender's worst case is a margin call. Borrowing against a tokenized stock is SBL where the collateral can refuse to transfer, the price can be stale by design, and the callable clause is replaced by an automated liquidation that never sleeps. Every layer between the wallet and the share is now a layer between the lender and recovery.

No issuer documents a lending, margin, or collateral path for tokenized equity, and no protocol venue is verified live, as of 2026-09-07 — the Dinari doc index carries no such page and Hyperliquid's dShare integration is deployment, not collateral. This rubric is therefore forward-looking in full: the method for judging any venue the moment one appears, and the questions to put to it before quoting an LTV. Treat any "borrow against your dShares" product quoted today the way the strip rubric treats a quoted strip price: name the venue and the contract, or call it fabricated.

## The menu: what breaks first (ranked)

1. **The recovery path — collateral that can refuse to move.** On default, the lender's remedy is seizing the token, and the token's transfer is eligibility-gated: policy scopes, KYC, registration at the issuer. A lender who cannot legally hold the collateral holds a broken remedy — the asset exists and cannot be delivered. The first question to any venue: on default, who ends up holding the token, and is that recipient eligible to hold it? TradFi SBL never asks this; a share transfers to anyone. Onchain, the question is load-bearing.

2. **The clock mismatch — liquidation never sleeps, the collateral does.** Onchain lending liquidates around the clock on a price feed; the collateral's price follows the underlying's sessions (order placement 24/5, weekends only for the eligible subset) and the peg breathes around market-hours boundaries. A liquidation in the Sunday gap executes against a stale peg on the thinnest book of the week. A TradFi margin desk widens requirements before a known gap; a venue that has not coded that caution inherits the tail.

3. **Where the dividend lands during the term.** The dividend is paid to the registered holder at the issuer's snapshot, and registration is wallet-tied. Collateral pledged in place (borrower's wallet under allowance) keeps paying the borrower — the lender holds price-only collateral whether it knows so or not. Collateral moved to escrow or lender custody changes the payee — and whether an escrow wallet qualifies as registered is exactly the kind of question no venue has answered yet, because none exists. Ask it before entering, not after.

4. **LTV that credits the yield leg.** A collateral marked at price alone undervalues a high-yield underlying; one that credits the dividend stream must model the net pass-through — fraction, lag, and the $0.10-per-distribution floor (corporate-action.md) — never the headline yield. And the "future yield" in any product's name is a forecast: a dividend cut shrinks the collateral's yield leg without a single tick of price, a direction the oracle never watches. An LTV that credits yield has implicitly written an insurance policy on the dividend; price it that way.

5. **Corporate actions inside the term.** A split rebases the token through a halt-rebase-verify-resume cycle while it is paused — does a paused token still feed the price feed, and does a pledged amount scale with the rebase automatically or need re-approval? A cash merger extinguishes the position into a cash entitlement: the collateral silently becomes USD+, an asset the loan was never priced against, resolved on the issuer's manual-triage clock rather than the liquidation engine's. Any tenor spanning a dividend calendar or a live deal rumor carries this option; read corporate-action.md Layer A before choosing a term.

6. **Which leg is pledged.** A spot dShare carries price plus dividend and therefore the entire menu above. A PT, were strips to exist, is price-only collateral with cleaner math. A YT is a stream, not an asset — lending against it is lending against a forecast with an expiry, collateral that self-extinguishes at term end. The leg determines which entries of this menu even apply; establish it first when the instrument is a strip.

Questions that separate a designed venue from a wrapper: who is the eligible recipient of seized collateral; what feed triggers liquidation and what does it read when the underlying market is closed; where does the dividend land mid-term; is the yield leg in the LTV, at what fraction and what floor; what happens to the pledge through a rebase or a merger; and which leg is pledged at all?
